# Compound interest and loans

## Compound interest
Compound interest earns interest on previously earned interest. With annual rate r compounded n times per year for t years, a principal P grows to P * (1 + r/n)^(n*t). Time matters more than most people expect: money invested for 30 years at 7 percent roughly doubles every ten years.

## Regular contributions
Adding a fixed amount each month grows the balance by the future value of an annuity: contribution * (((1 + i)^m - 1) / i), where i is the monthly rate and m the number of months. Starting early with small amounts often beats starting late with larger ones.

## Rule of 72
Divide 72 by the annual percentage return to estimate the years needed to double money. At 6 percent, money doubles in about 12 years.

## Inflation and real returns
Real return is approximately nominal return minus inflation. A 5 percent return with 3 percent inflation grows purchasing power by only about 2 percent.

## Amortised loans
A fixed-rate loan has a constant payment. Early payments are mostly interest, later payments mostly principal. The monthly payment equals principal * i * (1 + i)^m / ((1 + i)^m - 1) where i is the monthly rate and m the number of payments. Paying extra principal early saves substantial interest.

## Fees matter
A fund fee of 1 percent a year may sound small but, compounded over decades, can consume a large share of final wealth compared with a 0.1 percent fee.
