# Valuation basics

## Price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio
P/E is the share price divided by earnings per share over the last twelve months (trailing) or the next twelve months (forward). A high P/E means investors pay a lot per dollar of earnings, usually because they expect fast growth. A low P/E may signal cheapness or a business in trouble. P/E is only meaningful when compared with similar companies or the company's own history.

## Market capitalisation
Market capitalisation is share price times shares outstanding. It measures the market value of the equity, not the value of the business. Large caps are generally more stable than small caps.

## Earnings per share and revenue growth
EPS is net income divided by shares outstanding. Revenue growth shows whether the business is expanding. Profit growth that comes from cost cutting alone is limited, while sustained revenue growth is harder to fake.

## Dividend yield
Dividend yield is annual dividends per share divided by the share price. A very high yield can be a warning that the market expects the dividend to be cut.

## Discounted cash flow
A discounted cash flow model estimates a company's value as the sum of its future cash flows, each discounted back to today at a rate that reflects risk. Small changes in the discount rate or growth assumptions move the result a lot, so treat the output as a range, not a precise number.

## Balance sheet health
Debt-to-equity compares borrowing with shareholders' funds. High leverage amplifies gains and losses and raises the risk of distress when interest rates rise.
