# Technical indicators: what they measure and where they fail

## Moving averages (SMA and EMA)
A simple moving average (SMA) is the mean closing price over the last N days. An exponential moving average (EMA) weights recent prices more. Price above its 50-day average is commonly read as an uptrend. When a short average (10-day) crosses above a long one (50-day), traders call it a bullish cross. Moving averages lag price by construction, so they confirm trends late and produce many false signals in sideways markets.

## RSI (Relative Strength Index)
RSI compares the size of recent gains to recent losses over 14 days and is scaled from 0 to 100. Readings above 70 are conventionally called overbought and below 30 oversold. In a strong trend RSI can stay above 70 for weeks, so "overbought" is not a sell signal by itself.

## MACD
MACD is the difference between a 12-day and a 26-day EMA. The signal line is a 9-day EMA of MACD, and the histogram is MACD minus the signal line. A positive histogram means short-term momentum is stronger than its recent average. It is a momentum gauge, not a forecast.

## Bollinger Bands
Bands sit two standard deviations above and below a 20-day moving average. Price near the upper band means it is high relative to recent volatility. Bands widen when volatility rises. Touching a band is not a reversal signal on its own.

## Volatility
Volatility is the standard deviation of returns. Daily volatility is annualised by multiplying by the square root of 252 trading days. Higher volatility means a wider range of outcomes, in both directions.

## Caveat
Decades of research show that simple technical rules have little or no reliable predictive power after costs for liquid, widely followed assets. Treat indicators as a description of recent behaviour, and test any rule out-of-sample before trusting it.
