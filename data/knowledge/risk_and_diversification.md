# Risk, diversification and portfolio basics

## Risk is the spread of outcomes
Risk in finance usually means volatility: how widely returns vary around their average. It is not the same as the chance of losing money, but the two are related. Higher expected return generally requires accepting higher risk.

## Diversification
Holding many assets that do not move in lockstep reduces unsystematic risk, the risk specific to one company or sector. It does not remove systematic (market) risk. Correlation measures how closely two assets move together: +1 is lockstep, 0 is unrelated, -1 is opposite. Combining assets with low correlation lowers portfolio volatility for the same expected return.

## Sharpe ratio
The Sharpe ratio is the average return above the risk-free rate divided by the standard deviation of returns. It measures return per unit of risk. A higher Sharpe ratio is better, but it is estimated from historical data and can be misleading when returns are skewed or the sample is short.

## Maximum drawdown
Maximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough fall in value. A 50 percent drawdown needs a 100 percent gain to recover. Drawdown shows how painful a strategy can be, which volatility alone hides.

## Position sizing
Putting too much in one asset makes a single bad outcome costly. A common guideline is to limit any single position to a small share of the portfolio. Never invest money you will need in the short term.

## Time horizon
Stocks have been far more volatile than bonds or cash over one year, but over decades broad stock indices have historically delivered higher average returns. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
